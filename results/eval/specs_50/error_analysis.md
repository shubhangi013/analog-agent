### Reading the single comparison table

- Each row names its JSONL trace relative to this report. `L123` means the actual 1-based line in that row's trace; `S123` is its `select_survivors` event. Model-call line numbers are in the latency column.
- Every listed candidate passed range admission, and every candidate has a typical DC event. There are **no range-only or range-rejected accepted candidates** in this subset.
- `T`, `HL`, `CL`, `HH`, `CH` mean `typical` (27 C, 1.8 V), `hot_low_supply` (125 C, 1.62 V), `cold_low_supply` (-40 C, 1.62 V), `hot_high_supply` (125 C, 1.98 V), `cold_high_supply` (-40 C, 1.98 V).
- The actual gated order is range -> T/DC -> T/AC -> HL/DC -> HL/AC -> CL/DC -> CL/AC -> HH/DC -> HH/AC -> CH/DC -> CH/AC. An entry gives the **furthest observed stage**; all preceding gates passed. `ALL 5 PASS` means all five DC/AC condition pairs passed.
- `+` means that stage passed; `-` means it failed. `D` means a typical AC pass was explicitly deferred by survivor selection and never received extra-condition tests. `B` means no remaining budget/admission capacity for the next stage, not an electrical failure. Deferred or untested candidates are **not** counted as verified designs.
- `Sat(M5)` means measured `vds < vdsat`: M5 is not saturated. Extra-condition failures include the actual `vds < vdsat` values in volts. `INV(M1,M2)` means the verifier's "device is off or below the strong-inversion gate" for both devices; it does not assert which of those two interpretations applies.
- `G`, `BW`, `Pdc` are measured gain in dB, unity-gain bandwidth in Hz, and DC power in W. Inequalities show the matched target. `CAL` is a simulator error, not an electrical verdict: ngspice reports `measure bias_voltage find(AT): out of interval`, the requested `i(vsense)` crossing is unavailable, and `bias_voltage` is then undefined. All 10 `CAL` events are in the random arms.
- `Planner / populations` separates continuation planning rounds from evaluated six-candidate populations, which include r0. Agent continuation rounds are model calls; random continuation rounds are `random_candidates` calls. Initial population generation is not fallback.

## Failure groups and counts

These are **mutually exclusive primary analytical groups for the 10 failed agent arms**, not the trace's stop-reason labels:

- **Deadline/latency: 0.** All task starts declare a 300-second timeout; all agent wall times are below 98 seconds. Latency is expensive, but no selected case stopped on a deadline.
- **Fallback: 0.** All 20 agent model calls were valid on attempt 1; both planner rounds supplied accepted model candidates. No continuation used generated fallback candidates.
- **Poor initial population: 0 primary; 7 contributing.** E06, E08, E10, M02, M03, M05 and M10 have no typical-target-passing r0 candidate. They subsequently find typical passes, so initial population quality alone is not the strongest explanation of their terminal failures.
- **Planner stuck on near-miss: 9.** E01, E02, E06, E07, E08, E10, M02, M03, M05 repeatedly reach typical feasibility but lose selected designs at extra-condition M5 saturation gates. Those DC failures are omitted from subsequent planner feedback. This means **feedback-starved near-misses**, not proof that the model ignored evidence it received, repeated identical candidates, or emitted a `stalled` stop.
- **Other: 1.** M10 has visible hot-low-supply AC bandwidth failures, followed by six new typical passes with no remaining corner admission capacity. The traces do not establish that the revised candidates still fail bandwidth, so it is classified as budget/corner starvation rather than a proven repeated near-miss.

There are overlapping contributors: **all 10** have final-round corner starvation, and **7** have a weak initial population as defined above. These overlapping counts must not be added to the exclusive primary counts.

### Accounting and misleading fallback labels

The agent arms use **376 executions** and the matching random arms **239**. Agent candidate terminal states are: 20 typical DC rejections, 33 typical AC rejections, 20 extra-condition DC rejections (11 HL, 9 CL), 5 HL AC rejections, 84 typical passes explicitly deferred, and 18 budget-limited candidates (12 DC passes without AC, 6 AC passes without corners). Random terminal states are: 80 typical DC rejections, 1 typical AC rejection, 9 extra-condition DC rejections (5 HL, 2 CL, 2 HH), 1 HL AC rejection, 7 deferred typical passes, and 10 fully verified candidates. These sum to 108. The typical DC rejections include the 10 explicitly recorded calibration errors; a successful random run does not mean every simulation succeeded. Total recorded planner latency is **381.139 s**. Summed task wall times are **674.986 s agent** versus **189.775 s random**; these are sums of overlapping task durations, not elapsed batch makespan. None of these timings includes shared intake or later judging.

Do not use `results.json.fallback_calls` as the count of fallback rounds: `eval\run_eval.py:227-230` counts `make_candidates` tool calls, including the agent's initial population. ## Single most likely root cause

**The planner's feedback loop drops DC gate failures, so it cannot see the dominant measured reason its near-miss designs are rejected.**

The evidence is structural and present in the saved runs:

1. `Orchestrator._rows` explicitly skips every result key ending in `|op` (`agent\orchestrator.py:742-755`). `_planner_state` builds both `best` and `latest` from those AC-only rows (`agent\orchestrator.py:758-781`). DC failures therefore never become planner result rows.
2. `_evaluate` also builds round summaries from `_rows` (`agent\orchestrator.py:568-594`). `round_line` counts passing condition rows and issues in those rows (`agent\context.py:229-245`), not complete candidate designs. Consequently, E01's r1 summary says `6 simulated, 6 passed ... failures: none` despite r1c5 failing M5 saturation at HL/DC (L226); the next model call is L278. E08 likewise reports `6 simulated, 6 passed ... failures: none` despite both selected candidates failing HL/DC (L149, L152); its next call is L187.
3. Before r2, all nine near-miss cases have saved extra-condition DC failures absent from the model's measured context. Eight of their r1 summaries report `failures: none`; M03 reports only its typical gain failure. The r2 hypotheses keep discussing gain/bandwidth changes or unspecified all-condition feasibility, not the measured M5 saturation failure. For example, E10's next hypothesis discusses hot-low bandwidth headroom even though its selected survivor's terminal rejection is CL/DC M5 saturation.
4. I reconstructed the bounded r2 context from each saved pre-call checkpoint using the source-hash-matching context builder. Reconstructed system-plus-user character counts match all 10 recorded `context_chars`: E01 4188, E02 4298, E06 4187, E07 4295, E08 4194, E10 4235, M02 4236, M03 4382, M05 4187, M10 4493. None includes a measured `not saturated` failure. This is a reconstruction, not an original prompt capture: the trace stores context lengths and hypotheses, not full optimization messages.

**Budget/survivor scheduling amplifies this feedback defect.** `_simulate` spends DC and AC executions on the whole fresh population before admitting corners (`agent\orchestrator.py:404-438`). Admission reserves eight executions per new survivor: `remaining // (2 * (len(CORNERS) - 1))`. Failed selected survivors are removed, but deferred candidates are not backfilled (`agent\orchestrator.py:439-451`). `_propose` creates six fresh candidate IDs for the next round rather than carrying those deferred survivors forward (`agent\orchestrator.py:331-376`).

E08 is an explicit example: r0 uses 9 executions; r1 finishes at 23 after both selected candidates fail their first extra DC probe. Four already-measured typical passes remain deferred. The next fresh population consumes another 12 executions, leaving 5, so all six new typical passes are deferred at S271 and the run stops as `budget_exhausted`. M10 has the same terminal budget pattern without a hidden extra-condition DC failure: r1 ends at 25, r2 consumes 12, and the remaining 3 cannot admit a survivor.

Four agent runs consume all 40 executions. Six stop below the nominal limit because fewer than eight remain: E06 leaves 6, E08 5, E10 4, M03 1, M05 5, M10 3. `agent\orchestrator.py:450-451` sets `exhausted` when no new survivor can be admitted; `_stop_reason` then returns `budget_exhausted` (`agent\orchestrator.py:596-598`). Thus an early budget stop here is not evidence of a latency deadline.

This identifies the strongest common mechanism, not a counterfactual guarantee: the saved traces cannot establish that a deferred candidate would have passed all corners, or how much restoring feedback would improve success. The agent/random comparison also differs in initialization and predictor ranking, not only model use (`eval\run_eval.py:279-289`); it is not an isolated causal ablation.

## Why A04 asked for clarification

Source: `at least 30dB gain, 20MHz bandwidth, 60deg phase margin, 200uW max`.

The exact chain is:

1. **The deterministic parser rejects quantity-before-label syntax.** `agent\spec.py:31-35` expects forms such as `gain ... 30dB`, `bandwidth ... 20MHz`, `phase margin ... 60deg`, and `power ... 200uW`. A04 instead puts quantities before labels and omits the `power` label. The patterns already allow missing spaces between quantities and units, so lack of spaces is not the reason. Unconsumed numbers trigger `agent\spec.py:53-59`: `if re.search(r"\d|[<>]", remainder): raise ValueError(...)`. A read-only local parser call reproduces `Unrecognized numeric constraint. Use gain >= N dB, UGF >= N MHz, phase margin >= N deg, and power <= N uW.`
2. **Auto routing invokes model intake after that parse failure.** `route_request` catches the parser's `ValueError` and returns the original goal for interpretation (`agent\intake.py:74-84`). Trace: `A04_intake\A04_intake.jsonl`, L1-L2.
3. **The intake prompt encourages questions for ambiguity.** `agent\intake.py:35-42` asks for a complete source clause including comparison, forbids omitting requirements or using defaults for ambiguous supplied targets, and explicitly says: `Ask concise questions for ambiguous, conflicting, unsupported or unitless requirements.`
4. **The model elects to ask two questions.** The valid first-attempt `IntakeReply` at L3 binds only gain and power and asks: `Does "20MHz bandwidth" mean a minimum unity-gain bandwidth?` and `Is "60deg phase margin" a minimum requirement?` Model-call latency is **13.906 s**.
5. **The exact mandatory stop rule is any nonempty question list.** `bound_request`, `agent\intake.py:90-92`: `if proposal.questions: raise ClarificationNeeded("Please clarify: " + " ".join(proposal.questions))`. `interpret_request` explicitly accepts question-bearing structured replies in its validation callback and then invokes `bound_request` (`agent\intake.py:178-182`). L4 records `clarification_required`, **0 simulations**, and **13.955 s** wall time.

Importantly, code does **not** specifically require a question whenever a phase-margin minimum is implicit or the word `bandwidth` appears. The direction check rejects reversed comparisons but does not require an explicit lower-bound token for non-power constraints (`agent\intake.py:117-128`). A read-only `bound_request` call with four complete source bindings and no questions accepts the same source as gain >= 30 dB, UGF >= 20 MHz, PM >= 60 deg, power <= 200 uW. Therefore A04's stop is the combination of the label-first parser limitation, the model's ambiguity judgment under the intake prompt, and the nonempty-question stop rule--not simulator infeasibility or a hard-coded ban on those quantities.
